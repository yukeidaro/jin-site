Restating the problem

This is not a search speed problem.

Making search faster solves none of it, because you don't know the words to search for. You didn't write the document. A 2008 longitudinal study (Bergman et al., ACM TOIS) found people reach files by navigating 56–68% of the time and searching only 4–15% — and improving search barely moved that number. Search is what people do when everything else has failed.

What Jin does

Give it a cover. Fold it down. Keep it traceable.

Three things. Nothing conceptually new — this is what people have done with books and shelves for centuries, done automatically.

01

Covermake it recognisable

Every document gets a cover you can identify at a glance. The first pass is generated without reading the file — name, type, date, location only — so it appears instantly. The read-through pass swaps in quietly afterwards.

Constraint: the swap must not move row order or row height
02

Foldcollapse the pile

2,650 items are never shown as 2,650 items. They fold into 9 labelled groups. Colour alone only says "these belong together" — it never says what they are — so the group name is always spelled out in words.

Gate: 9 of 10 people answer correctly in a 5-second test
03

Tracesurvive the rewrite

Every paragraph, slide and cell range carries a stable ID. When an AI rewrites the document, you can still see exactly what changed — at the level of meaning, not file-level diffs.

Gate: 99% ID survival across a round trip

The product

Three ways to look at the same workspace.

Nothing here asks you to learn Markdown, adopt a syntax or migrate anything. It is a notebook. The structure is carried by colour and position, so you find things by recognising them rather than by remembering their names.

Notebook

Everything in its place

Section spines give every group a colour and a name. Page ribbons carry that colour into the document, so you always know where you are without reading a breadcrumb.

Focus

One continuous surface

The page and the native files inside it read as a single document. A live PowerPoint slide is not an attachment sitting at the bottom — it is part of the paragraph that argues for it.

Project

The whole thing at once

The same colours become coordinates. Overview, meetings, research and deliverables lay out as one map, which is the folded state of an entire project.

The gate

From install to the first "there it is" — 60 seconds.

Five minutes is a number your operating system's search already beats. It looks like a bar but decides nothing. 60 seconds forces the design. Here is what it spends, and what each budget rules out.

30s
Launch, and pick one folder
→ rules out account creation, sign-in, onboarding and a settings screen
10s
From folder picked to list on screen
→ rules out a loading spinner. Results must stream in from the first row
20s
From list on screen to finding the one
→ 20s is scanning 10 things with your eyes. It is not reading 100
v1 makes no network calls

What accumulates is not files. It's the rules.

One person installs it, and their own machine gets cleaner and more legible. That alone is the whole value — no company-wide rollout required, no approval needed. Turn sync on later and the default is still that only the rules you authored leave the machine. Not the names. Not the contents.

Non-goals

What Jin is not.

Written before what it is. Products that leave this vague try to become everything and end up as nothing.

NOT / 01
Not a search engineEverything and Spotlight are free and genuinely fast. We don't compete there. Jin is for the case where you don't know the words.
NOT / 02
Not cloud storageIt doesn't replace OneDrive, Drive or Box. v1 never touches the network — it works entirely on the disk you already have.
NOT / 03
Not another notes appIt adds no new place to write. It reads the 2,650 files already sitting on your disk. No migration, no import.
NOT / 04
Not a chatbotIt won't hand you a summary and call it done. The goal is that you see the whole shape yourself and choose for yourself.
