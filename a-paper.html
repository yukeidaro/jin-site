The problem

Markdown file chaos.

The more agents you use, the more Markdown appears in the background. These files are hard for business users to read, edit or organise — and every agent files things by its own logic. Run a few at once and you quickly lose track of what was created, what matters, and where any of it belongs.

Three agents, three conventions, one folder. Half of it was never written for you to read — instructions, memory, logs and intermediate output sit right next to the two documents that actually matter.

This is where the familiar tools start to give out. SharePoint and OneDrive were built for documents people write, not for the volume and variety agents produce. And Obsidian, which handles Markdown beautifully, assumes you are comfortable living in a vault of raw files and links — which most business users are not.

How it works

Two structures, kept in sync.

There is the way you want to understand and organise your work, and the way agents create and store files. Jin AI holds both, and keeps them aligned as the pile grows past what anyone can track by hand.

The agent structure

How files actually get written

  • Markdown on disk, unchanged
  • Each agent's own conventions
  • Instructions, memory and logs
  • Intermediate and throwaway output
Your decisions become the rule. When you move a project boundary, rename a category, hide something or link two things together, Jin AI treats that as a statement of intent — and keeps future agent-generated files aligned with it, automatically. The files themselves stay plain Markdown, so every agent can still read and write them.

The product

One workspace. Three ways to look at it.

Nothing asks you to learn Markdown or move your files anywhere. They stay exactly where they are, in the format your agents expect. Jin AI just draws them — so you find things by recognising them.

Notebook

Everything in its place

Projects and sections carry a colour and a name, and that colour follows the document you open. You always know where you are without reading a file path.

Focus

A document, not a text file

The same Markdown, rendered as a page you can actually read and edit — with slides, PDFs and sheets sitting inside the paragraph that needs them.

Project

The whole thing at once

The same colours become coordinates. A whole project — meetings, research, decisions and deliverables — lays out as one map you can take in without opening anything.

A visual editor for Markdown

Edit, reorganise, duplicate and connect content directly. Everything you do is written back as ordinary Markdown, so the next agent picks up exactly where you left off.

Agent-only files, out of sight

Instructions, memory, logs and intermediate output stay on disk and stay available to agents — they simply don't appear in your default view.

Compared to Obsidian

Obsidian is excellent. It just wasn't built for this.

Obsidian is a genuinely great Markdown editor and knowledge base, and we use it. But its core experience is built around individual notes, file lists and links, and it assumes you're comfortable managing a vault yourself.
