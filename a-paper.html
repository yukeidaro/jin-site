The problem

You lose the file twice.

First you forget what it was called. Then you forget where it went. When an agent starts a project it also decides where things should go — and unless you tell it exactly how to organise everything, the structure stops matching how you would have organised it yourself.

“I edited an MD file in Codex and wanted to use it in a meeting later. When I came back I couldn’t remember what the file was called, or where it was.”

“That is not where I would have put it.”

So the afternoon goes on moving files and cleaning up folders that an agent made in a second.

“You end up opening files one by one just to work out what you made.”

Agents create and edit quickly. The filenames and the folder structure are what get lost.

Three agents, three conventions, one folder. Half of it was never written for you to read — instructions, memory, logs and intermediate output sit right next to the deck you actually have to deliver.
3,950
One machine, one week. On our CEO’s laptop, 3,950 Markdown files were created in a single week. 89% were never opened again, and 283 were simply named README.md. Almost none of them were things anyone meant to create or needed to see — they were just left behind as agents worked.
The solution

Visible, editable, reversible.

Jin AI does not change how your agents work. It simply makes the structure easier to see, lets you correct it by hand, and lets agents help maintain it in the background.

See

See the whole project at once

Projects, sub-projects, folders and files are shown together in one view. You can see where a file belongs without opening folders one by one, or trying to remember what the agent named it.

Fix

Move things around like normal files

Rename a file. Move it. Change the folder. Reorganise a project. When an agent puts something in the wrong place you drag and drop it, instead of rebuilding the structure from scratch.

Undo

See what changed, and change it back

Changes made by you and changes made by agents are recorded separately. If something gets moved, renamed or reorganised the wrong way, you can see what happened and roll it back.

The agent can still reach everything. You just don’t need to see everything. Jin AI doesn’t delete anything or hide files from the agent. It gives people a cleaner view of the files that actually matter to them, while the agent keeps working with the full project in the background.

Your corrections don’t stop at the screen. Move a project boundary, rename a category or hide something, and Jin AI writes it back into the instruction files your agents already read — CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, copilot-instructions.md and the rest — so the next file an agent writes lands where you would have put it. No plugin and no API: just the Markdown they are reading anyway. You never open CLAUDE.md. Jin AI keeps it correct.

Why the structure, and not a better search box: people reach their files by navigating 56–68% of the time and by searching 4–15% — and that barely moved even after their search tools were upgraded. Bergman et al., ACM TOIS, 2008.

The product

What it looks like.

See your work as a workspace, then open a page and get to work. Nothing asks you to learn Markdown or move your files anywhere — they stay exactly where they are, in the format your agents expect. Jin AI just draws them, so you find things by recognising them.

The visual workspace

See the whole project at a glance

Pages and files stay organised in a structure that makes sense. Meetings, research, decisions and deliverables lay out as one map you can take in without opening anything.

The page

Everything about one thing, together

Notes, decisions, PowerPoint files, PDFs and links sit on a single page — all built on Markdown, so the next agent picks up exactly where you left off.

A visual editor for Markdown

Edit, reorganise, duplicate and connect content directly. Everything you do is written back as ordinary Markdown, so nothing you touch becomes unreadable to an agent.

Agent-only files, out of sight

Instructions, memory, logs and intermediate output stay on disk and stay available to agents — they simply don't appear in your default view.

The outcome

Both sides get what they need.

The point isn't a tidier folder. It's that the two audiences for your work stop pulling against each other.

People stop managing files

You spend your attention on moving the work forward instead of hunting for the right version, renaming things and deciding where they belong. The structure is still there — you just aren't the one maintaining it.

AI gets richer context

Agents can see relationships, sources and history across everything you've produced, without that same volume being pushed at you. More context for them does not have to mean more noise for you.
