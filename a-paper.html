The gap

Today you choose: familiar, or connected.

Both halves of the modern workspace already exist. Neither one is willing to be the other, so most teams live in both and pay the tax of moving between them.

Where the work lands

Familiar, but disconnected

Folders, projects and documents. Slides, docs and spreadsheets are where business outcomes are actually produced — and they are the things people can navigate without being taught.

FoldersPowerPointWordExcelPDF
But the reasoning behind each one lives somewhere else entirely.
Where the thinking lives

Connected, but unfamiliar

Linked notes give you genuinely AI-friendly, connected knowledge. But Markdown, backlinks and a deliberately sparse interface are designed for technical and power users — not for the person producing the deck.

MarkdownBacklinksGraph viewPlugins
Powerful, and quietly out of reach for most of the team.
This is not a file-management problem. Work in the AI era should not be fragmented by application or by file type. What is missing is an interaction layer — a visual workspace that holds knowledge, Office documents and AI-generated work together, and still looks like the workspace people already know.

The product

One workspace. Three ways to look at it.

Nothing asks you to learn a syntax, adopt a taxonomy or migrate anything. Your files stay where they are and keep their names. JinAI draws them, so you find things by recognising them — the way you already work.

Notebook

Everything in its place

Projects and sections carry a colour and a name, and that colour follows the document you open. You always know where you are without reading a path.

Focus

The document and its reasoning

A live slide sits inside the paragraph that argues for it. Notes, sources and decisions read as one continuous surface with the deck they produced — not as attachments at the bottom.

Project

The whole thing at once

The same colours become coordinates. A whole project — meetings, research, decisions and deliverables — lays out as one map you can take in without opening anything.

How it works

Familiar on the surface. Structured underneath.

AI does the structuring and connecting in the background. You never see a graph you have to interpret, and you are never asked to file something correctly for it to work.

Architecture

JinAI sits under the tools, not inside them.

It isn't a plugin for one assistant or one suite. JinAI works on the workspace itself — a convention plus an index that lives with your files — so anything that produces a document can feed it, and the way you structure work is honoured no matter which application or which model made the file.
