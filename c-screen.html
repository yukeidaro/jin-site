One — the problem

This is not a search speed problem.

Making search faster solves none of it, because you do not know the words to search for. You did not write the document. A 2008 longitudinal study found people reach files by navigating 56–68% of the time and searching only 4–15% — and improving search barely moved the number.

— and 48 more. All of it in 0.02 seconds. Speed was never the bottleneck.
I have to read all of it just to find out what's in there. Said out loud, twice, in one afternoon

208 files named README. 55 named DESIGN. 33 named lessons. Strip out every boilerplate file a framework generates and one in five still cannot be told apart by name.

Two — the method

Give it a cover. Fold it down. Keep it traceable.

Three things. Nothing conceptually new — this is what people have done with books and shelves for centuries, done automatically.

01

Covermake it recognisable

Every document gets a cover you can identify at a glance. The first pass is generated without reading the file — name, type, date, location — so it appears instantly. The read-through pass swaps in quietly afterwards.

Constraint: the swap must not move row order or height
02

Foldcollapse the pile

2,650 items are never shown as 2,650 items. They fold into nine labelled panels. Colour alone only says "these belong together", never what they are — so the name is always spelled out in words.

Gate: nine of ten people answer correctly in a five-second test
03

Tracesurvive the rewrite

Every paragraph, slide and cell range carries a stable identifier. When an AI rewrites the document you can still see exactly what changed — at the level of meaning, not a file-level diff.

Gate: 99% identifier survival across a round trip

Three — the product

Three ways to look at the same workspace.

Nothing here asks you to learn Markdown, adopt a syntax or migrate anything. It is a notebook. The structure is carried by colour and position, so you find things by recognising them rather than by remembering their names.

Notebook view — section spines colour and name every group
Notebook

Everything in its place

Section spines give every group a colour and a name. Page ribbons carry that colour into the document, so you always know where you are without reading a breadcrumb.

Focus

One continuous surface

The page and the native files inside it read as a single document. A live slide is not an attachment at the bottom — it sits inside the paragraph that argues for it.

Project

The whole thing at once

The same colours become coordinates. Overview, meetings, research and deliverables lay out as one map — the folded state of an entire project.

Four — the gate

From install to the first "there it is" — sixty seconds.

Five minutes is a number your operating system's search already beats. It looks like a bar but it decides nothing. Sixty seconds forces the design. That is the entire point of the number.

Five — privacy

What accumulates is not files. It's the rules.

v1 makes no network calls. One person installs it and their own machine gets cleaner and more legible — that alone is the whole value, so no rollout and no approval is required. Turn sync on later and the default is still that only the rules you authored leave the machine.

Six — the limits

What Jin is not.

Written before what it is. Products that leave this vague try to become everything and end up as nothing.

NOT ONE
Not a search engineEverything and Spotlight are free and genuinely fast. We do not compete there. Jin is for the case where you do not know the words.
NOT TWO
Not cloud storageIt does not replace OneDrive, Drive or Box. v1 never touches the network — it works on the disk you already have.
NOT THREE
Not another notes appIt adds no new place to write. It reads the 2,650 files already sitting on your disk. No migration, no import.
NOT FOUR
Not a chatbotIt will not hand you a summary and call it done. The goal is that you see the whole shape yourself and choose for yourself.
