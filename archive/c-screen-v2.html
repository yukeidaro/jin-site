Two — the product

Three ways to look at the same folder.

Nothing here asks you to learn a syntax, migrate anything or move your files somewhere new. They stay exactly where they are. Jin just draws them, so you find things by recognising them instead of remembering their names.

Notebook

Everything in its place

Each group gets a colour and a name, and that colour follows the document you open. You always know where you are without reading a path.

Focus

One continuous surface

A page and the files it refers to read as a single document. A slide or a PDF is not an attachment at the bottom — it sits inside the paragraph that needs it.

Project

The whole thing at once

The same colours become coordinates. A whole project lays out as one map you can take in without scrolling or opening anything.

Three — the method

It follows your structure, not someone else's.

Most tools want you to adopt their taxonomy. Jin does the opposite: it reads the way you already break folders down, and applies that consistently to everything that lands afterwards — including everything your agents write.

01

Visualisesee the shape

Every file gets a cover you can recognise at a glance, generated from what is already there — name, type, date, neighbours. No tagging, no filling in forms.

02

Break downyour rules, applied

Jin learns the folder breakdown you already use and keeps applying it. New files land where you would have put them, and the grouping stays stable as the folder grows.

03

Surfaceonly what's worth reading

Agent scratch files, near-duplicates, machine output and dead drafts fold away by default. What is left on top is the layer a human should actually read.

Four — architecture

Jin sits under the agents, not inside them.

Jin is not a plugin for one assistant. It works on the folder — a convention plus an index that lives on disk — so anything that can write a file can feed it. Change your tools next month and nothing about your workspace has to change.

Five — privacy

Your files stay on your machine.

Jin reads your folders locally. There is no upload step and no bucket somewhere with your documents in it. If you later turn on sync across your own devices, the default is still that only your organising rules travel — never the names, never the contents.
